\chapter{Conclusions and Outlook for Semiconductor-Centric EMC Characterization}    
\label{ch:Conclusion}
\section{Summary}


This paper presented a measurement environment for characterizing intrinsic electromagnetic emissions of power semiconductor devices directly at their power terminals.
The setup combined dual-channel active voltage sensing (low-pass and high-pass path), an active current shunt, and dedicated post-processing methods to enhance the measurability of the EMC-relevant high-frequency range.

The results showed that conventional probing became noise-limited at about \SI{115}{MHz} for the investigated switching transient.
By adding the high-pass path, the usable range was extended to about \SI{220}{MHz}.
With the time-dependent filter (TDF), comparative analysis became possible up to approximately \SI{500}{MHz}; however, only the range up to \SI{340}{MHz} was fully verified, and results above this limit should be interpreted comparatively rather than as absolute calibration.
This enabled event-resolved spectral analysis of both switching voltages and the commutation current.

The application examples confirmed the relevance for semiconductor benchmarking: for the investigated chip-size variation, broadband differences of up to \SI{14}{dB} were measured in the voltage spectrum, and the market comparison showed device-to-device differences of up to about \SI{7}{dB} in average EM emission levels.
These findings demonstrate that EM emission behavior provides an additional design dimension in semiconductor development beyond the classical trade-off of conduction losses, switching losses, and short-circuit robustness.

\textit{Remaining limitations:} The main remaining constraints are (1) induced noise by operational amplifiers, especially in the current measurement path at higher frequencies, which degrades SNR above \SI{270}{MHz}; (2) probe verification uncertainty above \SI{340}{MHz}; and (3) sensitivity to parasitic coupling from the switching node, which required careful shielding design. 
Future work will therefore focus on reducing added noise through lower-noise operational amplifiers, improving the common-mode robustness, extending the verification setup to at least \SI{500}{MHz} using alternative band-pass filters, and benchmarking alternative sensor architectures (e.g., purely passive designs) with respect to noise floor, bandwidth, isolation performance, and implementation effort.
