\section{Design}
Measuring the EMC-relevant high-frequency drain-source voltage components requires a probe with low noise, high bandwidth, and a short, well-defined electrical connection to the DUT terminals.
In addition, the output interface must provide a \SI{50}{\ohm} output for supplying both a low-pass and a high-pass signal path for time-domain and EMC-oriented frequency-domain evaluation, respectively.

To the best of our knowledge, no commercially available probe fully met these requirements. 
In particular, low output noise is often not a primary design target in conventional probe designs. 
Instead, many commercial probes use a large input voltage divider to keep the operational amplifier input swing small and thereby extend the bandwidth. 
However, this architecture, together with the subsequent amplification stage, can degrade the overall noise performance.

\subsection{Unity Gain}


Therefore, a custom dual-channel differential active voltage probe (DAVP), originally introduced in~\cite{kragl_active_2025}, is used and further refined in this work. %, which is fully verified up to at least \SI{350}{MHz}.

In contrast to conventional differential probes, the DAVP splits the signal after a unity-gain stage and provides a subtractor circuit with a dedicated second-order high-pass channel in parallel to the low-pass subtractor.
Compared with the previous version in~\cite{kragl_active_2025}, the updated design includes an additional shielding layer in a six-layer PCB at the $CR$ input, a trimmable HV PCB capacitor (by adjusting the effective electrode area), and a fully enclosed aluminum housing to further reduce parasitic coupling.
The high-pass channel amplifier was also changed to a fully differential amplifier, with reduced added noise and improved common-mode rejection.
