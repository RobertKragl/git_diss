\chapter{Sensor and Platform Design for Methodological Implementation}
\label{ch:Sensors}

The methodology introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:Methods} requires dedicated instrumentation to resolve the EMC-relevant, high-frequency components of the drain-source voltages and the commutation current of a switching cell for the EMC-relevant frequency range.
This chapter presents the hardware developed to realize this methodology: a dual-channel differential active voltage probe for the high-side and low-side switching-node voltages, a complementary dual-channel active current probe for the commutation current, and the measurement platform that integrates both sensors into a representative power-electronic application.

\section{Dual-Channel Differential Active Voltage Probe}
\label{ch:voltage}

Measuring the EMC-relevant high-frequency drain-source voltage components requires a probe with low noise, high bandwidth, and a short, well-defined electrical connection to the DUT terminals. 
The output interface must also provide a \SI{50}{\Omega} connection to supply both low-pass and high-pass signal paths for time-domain and EMC-oriented frequency-domain evaluation, respectively.

When the probe requirements were defined, no commercially available probe was known to meet them in full. 
However, during the development of this work, several manufacturers introduced a new generation of optically isolated voltage probes that appear promising. 
However, their linearity in transferring the small voltage components of interest here has not yet been tested. 
Furthermore, some optical probes transmit a digitized signal and reconstruct it with a DAC at the receiving end, reintroducing quantization noise that again limit the resolution of small high-frequency components. 
For the measurement approach pursued here, analog signal transmission is a strict requirement.

With previous differential probes, a challenge is the requirement for low output noise. 
This is often not a primary design target in conventional probe architectures due to its unimportance for conventional switching metrics.
To extend bandwidth while limiting the operational-amplifier input swing, these probes often use a large input voltage divider. 
The resulting signal attenuation requires greater gain in subsequent stages, which can degrade the probe's overall noise performance.

\section{Dual-Channel Active Current Probe}
\label{ch:AShunt}

While at lower frequencies the capacitive coupling from the switching node potential to the cooler dominates EMC concerns, recent studies indicate that inductive couplings become significant at higher frequencies as well~\cite{hamami_identifying_2026}.
Therefore, it is necessary to measure not only the high-frequency components of the voltage transient but also of the current transient.
