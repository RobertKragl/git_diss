\section{Requirements}

For the current measurement some requirements must be defined for a measurement in respect to EMC.
First-same as for the voltage measurement- a bandwidth of \SI{500}{MHz} or higher is needed.
This makes the use of  commercial Rogowski-coils impossible, furthermore rogoskis have the disadvantage to being prone to the positioning of the coil \cite{xin_review_2022}.

Furthermore, a galvanic isolated measurement is necessary, since the ground of the oscilloscope is fixed to the aluminum plate, which is also the ground of the LISNs.
Hence, classical passives shunts, which set the oscilloscope ground to DC+ or DC- are not feasible.
Lastly, the introduced measurement resistance must be one decade smaller than the \mi{R}{DS,on} of the DUT.
The presented measurement setup is designed for \SI{750}{V} and \SI{1200}{V} SiC-discretes in a D2PAK where the $\mi{R}{DS,on}~~\SIrange{20}{50}{m\ohm}$. (Cite PMTW05 and PMTX03 datasheets).
Therefore, the sense resistance must be \SI{2}{m\Omega} or smaller.
This is a problem for conventional shunts when the bandwidth is considered.
Every shunt resistance has some parasitic inductance and the bandwidth of the shunt corresponds to

\begin{equation}
    \mi{f}{bw,shunt} = \frac{1}{2\pi \tau_\text{shunt}} = \frac{\mi{R}{shunt}}{2\pi\mi{L}{shunt}} 
\end{equation}

Therefore, in classical shunt design higher bandwidths can be achieved with higher shunt resistance values. 
However, this is not feasible for the EMC measurement.
